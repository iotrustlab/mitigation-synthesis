This note explains the purpose of the cleaned Phase 1 artifact.

The current verified example is the canonical final-step case from `attack.envelope`.

Intuition:

1. The attacker controls the primary path during the active attack window and forces a harmful inflow command.
2. The defender has a redundant path that can still force `P1 := 0`.
3. The proof shows that one such intervention is enough to preserve safety and keep both cost metrics nonnegative in the final control period before the attack ends.

Why this matters:

- it gives one end-to-end verified mitigation certificate
- it confirms the metric accounting is internally consistent for this case
- it creates a trustworthy baseline for later, more general synthesis

What is still future work:

- multi-step mitigation over a longer attack horizon
- richer actuator overrides
- automated synthesis of invariants, envelopes, or tactics
